# Narrative frameworks by deck type

Used in two ways:
- **Create mode** — pick the skeleton for the deck type, then adapt it to the content.
- **Review mode** — check which required beats are present, missing, weak or out of order.

The 10-step pitch structure is the most complete skeleton here, and many of its beats (hook, problem, solution, proof, ask) are useful in almost any deck. But it was built for **investor pitches**. Applying it literally elsewhere produces wrong decks: a client steerco doesn't need TAM/SAM/SOM, and a keynote opening with a business model loses the room. Always start from the deck type, then borrow beats.

## 1. Universal beats

Almost every effective deck contains these, in some form:

| Beat | Question it answers for the audience |
|---|---|
| Hook | Why should I pay attention now? |
| Problem / situation | What is at stake, for whom? |
| Answer / solution | What do you propose? |
| Proof | Why should I believe it? (data, demo, cases, traction) |
| Ask / next step | What do you want from me, specifically? |

A deck missing the **ask** is the most common and most costly gap. "Questions?" is not an ask.

Cross-type practical rules:
- **Less is more** on presented slides; detail goes to speaker notes or appendix (thresholds in `density.md`).
- **Back claims with data** — every claim of size, growth, urgency or superiority needs a number and a source.
- **Tell a story** — context → tension → resolution, not a list of facts (`storyline.md`).
- **Adapt the language to the audience** — investors, clients, partners, executives and designers want different proof and different vocabulary.
- **Prepare for questions** — anticipate the weakest points (business model, risks, assumptions) and build backup slides in the appendix.

## 2. Investor pitch (10–15 slides, fundraising)

| # | Beat | What the slide must do | Review flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook & intro** | Grab attention in seconds: provocative question, striking number, or a short story. Move the audience from passive to active listening. | Opens with company history or a logo slide with no hook |
| 2 | **Problem** | A real, urgent, widespread problem the target lives with, supported by data. The audience should think "yes, that's a serious problem". | Problem vague, not quantified, or only the founder's opinion |
| 3 | **Solution / value proposition** | What you do, said simply, and what makes it unique. | Feature list instead of value; uniqueness not stated |
| 4 | **Product / demo** | Make it tangible: demo, screenshots, MVP. | Only text descriptions of the product |
| 5 | **Why now** | The technological, market or regulatory shift that makes this the right moment. | Missing — frequently skipped and often asked by investors |
| 6 | **Market (TAM, SAM, SOM)** | Total, addressable and obtainable market, plus growth trends. Show room for a large, scalable business. | Only TAM ("a $50B market"); top-down numbers without a bottom-up SOM; no sources |
| 7 | **Competition & advantage** | Direct and indirect competitors; the "unfair advantage" or clear differentiator. | "We have no competitors"; a 2x2 where you're top-right by construction without explanation |
| 8 | **Business model** | How revenue is generated (subscription, commission, direct sale…) and main costs. | Pricing missing; unit economics absent |
| 9 | **Traction & financials** | Results achieved (users, revenue, partnerships, feedback) and realistic 3–5 year projections. | Hockey-stick projections without assumptions; vanity metrics |
| 10 | **Team & call to action** | Key people and why they are the ones to execute. Close with an explicit ask: amount and use of funds, strategic partner, or feedback. | Team listed without relevance; no amount; no use of funds |

Adaptation notes:
- If traction is the strongest asset, move it forward (after solution). Lead with your best card.
- Early-stage with no revenue: replace financials with milestones and validation evidence (pilots, LOIs, waitlist, interviews).
- Appendix: detailed financials, cap table, product roadmap, risk answers.

## 3. Sales / client / freelance proposal

Same persuasive logic, different proof. Drop TAM/SAM/SOM, competition analysis and 5-year financials — the client doesn't care about your market size.

1. **Hook** — a fact about *their* business (a metric, a pain they mentioned, a competitor move)
2. **Their problem / goal** — in their words, showing you understood the brief
3. **Insight** — what you see that they might not (the reason to choose you)
4. **Solution / approach** — what you'll do, phased
5. **Proof** — case studies, before/after, testimonials, relevant portfolio pieces
6. **Deliverables & timeline**
7. **Investment** — packages or pricing, framed against value
8. **Team / who you are** — brief, relevant
9. **Ask** — the specific next step (approval, a workshop date, a signature)

For a freelance designer proposal, visual quality and originality *are* proof: the deck is itself a sample of the work.

## 4. Elevator pitch (1–3 minutes)

Often no slides, or 3–5 at most. Five beats: **hook → problem → solution + uniqueness → one proof point → ask**. One number, one sentence each. Cut market, competition, model unless asked.

---

## 5. Consulting deliverable (steerco, business case, recommendation)

Answer-first (Pyramid Principle / SCQA). Executives want the conclusion before the evidence; a "hook" here is the headline finding, not a provocative question.

1. **Title** — topic, audience, date
2. **Executive summary** — the recommendation in one sentence, then 3–5 supporting points (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer)
3. **Context / baseline** — where we are, what was expected
4. **Analysis** — one insight per slide, action titles, evidence
5. **Options** (if a decision is needed) — compared on explicit criteria
6. **Recommendation** — what, why, impact
7. **Roadmap / next steps** — owners, dates, decisions needed
8. **Appendix** — methodology, detailed data, backup

Borrowed from the pitch: "why now" (urgency/cost of inaction) and an explicit ask (the decision requested from the steerco).

## 6. Internal update / status review

1. **Headline status** — on track / at risk / off track, in one sentence
2. **Progress vs plan** — milestones, metrics
3. **Issues & risks** — with mitigation and owner
4. **Decisions / support needed** — the ask
5. **Next period**

Short. No hook theatrics; clarity and honesty about risks matter more.

---

## 7. Keynote / talk / workshop

1. **Hook** — story, question, surprising fact, a strong image
2. **The big idea** — one sentence the audience should remember
3. **3 supporting points** — each with a story, example or demo
4. **Tension / turn** — the counterintuitive bit or the obstacle
5. **Callback & close** — return to the opening hook
6. **CTA** — what the audience should *do* or try afterwards

Workshops add: agenda, exercise slides with clear instructions and timers, recap. Data is supporting, not leading; one number per slide, huge.

---

## 8. Creative: portfolio, brand presentation, concept review

1. **Brief / context** — client, challenge, constraints
2. **Insight** — the observation the work is built on
3. **Concept** — the idea in one line, with a hero visual
4. **Execution** — key applications, shown large
5. **System / rationale** — type, color, grid, motion: the logic behind choices
6. **Impact / results** — if available (metrics, feedback, awards)
7. **Next steps / ask** — approval, feedback on directions, rollout

For concept reviews with multiple directions: present each direction with the same structure so they can be compared; state your recommendation.
