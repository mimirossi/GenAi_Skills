# Storyline & structure

The deck has to argue, not report. A reader who sees only the titles, in order, should be able to restate the argument and the ask.

## Checks

**1. Action titles, not topic labels**
A title states the insight of the slide as a sentence. A topic label names a subject.
- Topic label: "Market Analysis" · "Q4 Performance Overview" · "Customer Feedback"
- Action title: "Southeast Asia grew 34% last year, 3x faster than our current markets"
- Test: could the title be pasted onto a different deck unchanged? Then it's a label.
- Section dividers, agendas, title slides and appendix may use labels — don't flag those.
- For `keynote` and `creative` decks, action titles are not mandatory: a single strong word or image can carry a slide when a speaker supplies the argument. Judge whether *the sequence* still makes sense, not whether each title is a sentence.

**2. One idea per slide**
- A title with "and" joining two claims usually hides two slides.
- A slide that needs two takeaways to summarize is two slides.
- Content that doesn't support the title is noise on this slide (it may belong elsewhere).

**3. Vertical alignment — the storyline test**
List all titles in order. Check:
- Do they form a logical chain (situation → complication → resolution, or claim → evidence → implication → ask)?
- Are there jumps where a step of reasoning is missing?
- Are there repeats (same point made twice)?
- Does the sequence end on a decision, recommendation or clear next step with owner and date where relevant?
- For `read` decks this test is decisive — the deck must work without a speaker.

**4. Horizontal alignment — slide coherence**
Each slide's body proves its title. Flag:
- chart that shows something other than what the title claims
- title claims a number the chart/table doesn't show
- body that contradicts the title

**5. Narrative arc**
There should be a beginning (context, why now), a middle (tension, evidence), and an end (resolution, ask). A deck that is only a sequence of facts is a report.
- Executive summary (consulting/pitch, read decks): present early and states the recommendation in one sentence.
- Pitch: the problem must be felt before the solution appears.
- Keynote: one memorable throughline; a reprise or callback at the end is a sign of craft.

**6. Slide purpose by position**

| Slide | Should communicate |
|---|---|
| Title | topic, audience, date — not the conclusion |
| Executive summary | the main recommendation in one sentence |
| Context | baseline, expectation, status quo |
| Analysis | one insight + supporting evidence |
| Recommendation | specific next steps, owners, deadlines |
| Appendix | detail that would overload the main flow |

**7. Pacing**
- Roughly one slide per 1–2 minutes when presented; a slide that would take more than ~5 minutes to explain is doing too much.
- Detail that interrupts the argument belongs in the appendix.

## Score anchors

5 — titles alone tell the full story and end on a clear ask; every slide proves its title
4 — storyline clear; 1–2 label titles or one weak link
3 — argument recoverable but several label titles, a gap or a buried conclusion
2 — reads as a collection of facts; recommendation unclear or late
1 — no discernible argument or the conclusion contradicts the evidence

## How to report

Always include the "Storyline check": the titles listed in order, then 2–4 lines on whether they form an argument. Rewrite the weakest label titles as action titles (use numbers from the slide; never invent data — if the number isn't on the slide, write `[X%]`).
