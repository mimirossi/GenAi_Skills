# Visual hierarchy & layout

Hierarchy decides where the eye goes first, second, third. When everything competes equally, the audience doesn't know where to look.

## Hierarchy levers

- **Size** — the title or key number is the largest element; supporting labels are smaller.
- **Weight & color** — bold or accent draws the eye; grey recedes. Use emphasis for one thing per slide.
- **Position** — main message top (or top-left in left-to-right reading cultures); evidence below.
- **Contrast** — high contrast for what matters; low contrast signals secondary.
- **Isolation** — space around an element raises its importance.

Squint test: blur the slide image mentally (or downscale it). The first thing you see should be the thing the slide is about.

## Layout checks

- **Grid & alignment**: elements share edges and columns across slides; nothing is off by a few pixels. Titles sit at the same position on every slide.
- **Margins**: consistent safe area; nothing touches the edge unless intentionally full-bleed.
- **Spacing rhythm**: consistent gaps between related/unrelated groups (proximity shows relationships).
- **Repeated patterns**: similar slides use the same layout so the audience learns the structure (e.g. chart left, three bullets right).
- **Balance**: visual weight distributed deliberately; no accidental empty corners or crowded sides.
- **Reading order**: obvious path; numbered steps, arrows or Z/F pattern where flow matters.
- **Consistency**: same title size across slides; same font roles; no random size jumps (e.g. titles varying between 24 and 32pt). `deck_inventory.py` shows title size spread — more than one title size (outside section/cover slides) is a finding.

## Score anchors

5 — instant focal point on every slide; tight grid; consistent patterns
4 — one or two misalignments or weak focal points
3 — hierarchy unclear on several slides or inconsistent positions
2 — elements compete; alignment loose throughout
1 — no hierarchy; chaotic placement
