# Originality

Originality is the difference between a deck the audience remembers and one that blends into the last ten they saw. It is not decoration and not novelty for its own sake — it is **distinctiveness that serves the message**.

Originality is in tension with consistency and clarity. Resolve the tension through the deck profile:
- `consulting` — originality lives in the *thinking*: a sharp framing, a new way to cut the data, a memorable framework. Visual flourishes are not expected and can hurt credibility. Low weight.
- `pitch` — the audience sees dozens of similar decks; a distinctive concept or narrative device is a real advantage.
- `keynote` — memorability is the goal; one strong visual idea and one memorable moment matter a lot.
- `creative` — the deck *is* evidence of the author's design ability; generic execution is a serious weakness. Highest weight.

## Two layers to judge

### A. Conceptual originality (always applies)
- **Framing**: is there a point of view, a fresh angle, a provocative claim — or only expected statements?
- **Structuring device**: a metaphor, recurring motif, journey, before/after, or framework that ties the deck together.
- **Data angle**: a non-obvious comparison or reframing ("3x faster than our current markets" beats "grew 34%").
- **Memorable moment**: at least one slide built to stick (a reveal, a striking number alone on a slide, a powerful image, a demo).
- **Specificity**: real examples, real names, real numbers instead of generic claims.

### B. Visual originality (weight depends on profile and template status)
- **Template-default look**: unmodified stock PowerPoint/Keynote/Canva/Google Slides themes, default fonts, default chart styles → generic.
- **Visual concept**: is there a recognizable art direction (a color story, typographic voice, image treatment, graphic motif) that is specific to this topic/brand?
- **Cliché imagery**: stock handshakes, globes, gears, lightbulbs, puzzle pieces, rocket ships for "growth", target/arrow for "goals", people high-fiving, 3D figures.
- **Cliché diagrams**: generic SmartArt cycles, chevrons and pyramids used without meaning; icon-in-circle grids repeated on every slide.
- **AI-generic look**: purple-blue gradients, glassmorphism cards, glowing orbs, uniform rounded cards with an emoji/icon each, center-aligned everything — a pattern that now reads as default.
- **Coherence**: an original idea applied once and abandoned is weaker than a modest idea held throughout.

When a template is mandatory (e.g. corporate master), don't penalize the template itself; judge originality inside it (framing, data angles, image choices, diagram invention).

## Guardrails
- Originality never excuses unclear storyline, unreadable text or inaccessible contrast. If a creative choice hurts clarity, report it under the dimension it hurts and note the trade-off.
- Don't reward strangeness. Ask: does this choice make the message more memorable *or* only more unusual?
- Name the cliché specifically and propose a concrete alternative tied to the content (e.g. "replace the rocket stock photo on slide 3 with the actual growth curve, oversized, with the inflection point annotated").

## Score anchors

5 — distinctive concept and execution that serve the message; memorable
4 — clear point of view and some distinctive moments
3 — competent but expected; few memorable elements
2 — generic: template defaults, stock clichés, interchangeable with any deck on the topic
1 — clichés dominate and actively cheapen the message
